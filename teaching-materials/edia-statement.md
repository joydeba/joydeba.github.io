# Equity, Diversity, Inclusion, and Accessibility (EDIA) Statement

**Debasish Chakroborti (Joy), Ph.D.**  
Computer Science / Software Engineering  
https://joydeba.github.io/ · debasish.chakroborti@usask.ca  
August 2026

My approach to teaching, research mentoring, and academic service is grounded in the belief that excellence in computing education requires equity, inclusion, and meaningful accessibility. Students arrive with different educational backgrounds, identities, and lived experiences; my responsibility is to create learning environments where all students can participate, persist, and succeed.

## Equity in Learning Design

I establish equity through transparent learning outcomes, clear rubrics, staged assessments, and frequent formative feedback. These practices reduce hidden expectations and help students understand how to progress in rigorous technical courses. Fairness also means compassionate, consistent responses to individual circumstances while preserving learning outcomes.

## Inclusion in Classroom Practice

I foster inclusive classrooms by encouraging respectful dialogue, supporting question-asking, and validating diverse problem-solving approaches. In team-based projects, I structure collaboration to promote shared accountability and equitable participation. Across cohorts in Bangladesh and Canada, I have seen how diversity strengthens innovation and collaboration in technology.

## Accessibility as a Core Principle

I integrate accessibility into course planning through consistent module organization, clear communication of expectations, varied instructional resources (written guides, slides, labs, and walk-throughs), and responsiveness to accommodation needs in partnership with institutional supports.

## Ongoing Learning and Responsibility

I view EDIA and reconciliation work as ongoing responsibilities. Completing the Reconciliation Education Certificate and Four Seasons of Reconciliation (First Nations University of Canada) deepened my understanding of Indigenous history, the purpose of land acknowledgments, and the Truth and Reconciliation Commission’s Calls to Action. I apply this learning by building respectful classroom culture, selecting inclusive examples, and designing accessible course structures.

I live and work on Treaty 6 Territory and the Homeland of the Métis, and I strive to honor that relationship through continued learning, reflective practice, and concrete teaching and service actions.
