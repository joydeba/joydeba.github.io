# Statement of Commitment to Equity, Diversity, Inclusion, Decolonization, and Indigenization (EDIDI)

**Debasish Chakroborti (Joy), Ph.D.**  
Computer Science / Software Engineering  
https://joydeba.github.io/ · debasish.chakroborti@usask.ca  
August 2026

My academic and professional journey from Khulna University in Bangladesh to the University of Saskatchewan and the School of Computing and Digital Innovation at Saskatchewan Polytechnic has shaped my belief that education must be inclusive, equitable, and accountable to all communities. Equity, Diversity, Inclusion, Decolonization, and Indigenization (EDIDI) are interlinked principles that guide my teaching, research, and professional relationships.

**Equity** means recognizing that not all learners begin from the same place and ensuring fair access to success. In courses such as Cloud Computing and Blockchain, I design flexible, learner-centered activities that support students from diverse linguistic, cultural, and educational backgrounds through thoughtful course design, clear expectations, and compassion for individual circumstances.

**Diversity** has defined my career across countries and institutions. Working with students and colleagues from varied cultures, I have seen how diversity strengthens innovation and collaboration. I encourage multiple perspectives in class discussions and research supervision, recognizing different voices and lived experiences as sources of strength.

**Inclusion** is a daily practice beyond representation. As President of the Computer Science Council / CSGC and through leadership roles connected to the Graduate Students’ Association, I have promoted participatory decision-making, transparency, and belonging.

**Decolonization and Indigenization** are part of my ongoing learning. I live and work on Treaty 6 Territory and the Homeland of the Métis. Completing the Reconciliation Education Certificate and the Four Seasons of Reconciliation Professional Development Course offered by the First Nations University of Canada deepened my understanding of Indigenous history, traditions, and knowledge systems, including the meaning of land acknowledgments and the centrality of education to healing and social change.

I am applying these insights by integrating community-based and narrative examples into technical courses where appropriate, beginning classes with land acknowledgments and brief reflections on respect and community, and planning to invite Indigenous Knowledge Keepers to share perspectives following appropriate protocols. I will continue attending reconciliation workshops, participating in campus events such as Orange Shirt Day, and collaborating with colleagues experienced in Indigenization.

My goal is to create classrooms that are technically rigorous and grounded in respect, reciprocity, and relationship—spaces where every student feels belonging and purpose. Reconciliation is a lifelong commitment. I will keep learning, teaching, and leading with empathy so that EDIDI values remain at the heart of my work.
