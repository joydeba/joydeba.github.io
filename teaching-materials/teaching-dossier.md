## 1. Teaching Philosophy and Approach

My teaching is rigorous, inclusive, and professionally relevant. I design learning experiences so students connect computing and software engineering theory with implementation, justify design decisions using evidence, and build communication, collaboration, and ethical reasoning skills required in professional practice.

I use outcomes-based course design, active learning, and structured formative feedback. Classes typically progress from foundational concepts through guided labs and practical assignments to integrative projects. Students are expected not only to produce working solutions, but also to justify architectural and analytical decisions, communicate technical rationale, and engage in collaborative problem solving.

## 3. Evidence of Teaching Effectiveness

Evidence in this package includes institutional teaching records, sample course outlines, and student evaluation materials. Across offerings, student outcomes and feedback reflect clarity of explanation, responsiveness to learner needs, and practical relevance of assignments and labs.

Course evaluation evidence includes an end-of-course SLEQ report for CMPT 145 (University of Saskatchewan, Spring/Summer 2025). Representative student feedback themes include clear and accessible explanations for early-stage learners, responsive communication and flexibility, and a strong commitment to student learning. I also treat engagement design in lower-year cohorts as an ongoing improvement priority.

I use teaching evidence formatively to improve instructional sequencing, assessment design, active-learning prompts, and feedback quality, strengthening engagement and measurable learning outcomes over time.

## 4. Sample Syllabi and Course Materials

Included sample outlines and learning artifacts illustrate outcomes-based design, coherent assessment structures, and progressive skill development through labs and projects.

**CNET 184** (Data Communications and Networking): approximately 60 contact hours; assessment mix of labs, quizzes, assignment, and exams. Learning outcomes include network models and properties, TCP/IP services, monitoring and troubleshooting, and network security mechanisms.

**CWEB 280** (Internet Programming / Web Applications 2): approximately 64 contact hours; assessment through assignments and projects. Outcomes include server-side web development, secure authentication, state management, SQL-backed data handling, RESTful patterns, and full-stack project delivery using contemporary web frameworks and API workflows.

## 5. Teaching Innovation and Continuous Improvement

I continuously refine course delivery through reflective practice and evidence-based updates. I incorporate contemporary engineering workflows—version control, testing pipelines, reproducible development environments, and iterative milestone feedback—where pedagogically appropriate, so students build both conceptual depth and practical readiness for capstone work and professional practice.

I also introduce structured team processes and authentic technical tasks that mirror real software and systems workflows, while adjusting rubrics and classroom activities based on learner performance patterns.

## 6. Equity, Diversity, Inclusion, and Accessibility (EDIA)

I design courses with transparent expectations, accessible learning resources, structured rubrics, timely formative feedback, and inclusive participation structures. I foster classroom environments where diverse perspectives are welcomed and where students from varied learning backgrounds can thrive while maintaining academic rigor.

My mentoring approach emphasizes belonging, fairness, and support. I integrate responsible technology discussions—including ethics, accessibility, and social impact—into computing and software engineering learning. Ongoing professional learning includes Reconciliation Education / Four Seasons of Reconciliation, which informs respectful classroom culture, inclusive examples, and accessible course structures.
